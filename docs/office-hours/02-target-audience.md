# Target Audience

> Office Hours — Builder Mode
> Date: 2026-04-04
> Phase: B2 — Who Would You Show This To

## Context
Identifying who the first users are and what reaction the project is aiming for.

## Findings
Two audiences, working in tandem:

**1. Fellow streamers** — Other Twitch/YouTube streamers who'd adopt the timer for their own channels. The hook: seeing a one-button Stream Deck trigger that seamlessly starts a branded countdown on stream. The reaction target: "I need that on my stream."

**2. The user's own community** — Viewers who interact with the timer during live streams. They experience the timer as part of the stream, and their engagement validates the tool for the first audience.

The dual audience creates a natural growth loop: the user's viewers see the timer working → fellow streamers in the community ask what tool it is → adoption spreads organically.

The killer interaction that makes streamers want it: **Streamer.bot integration with Stream Deck triggering** — most existing timers require alt-tabbing or manual operation. This one is fire-and-forget.

## Key Takeaways
- Build for yourself first, but architect for shareability from the start
- The demo moment is the Stream Deck press → instant on-stream countdown
- Fellow streamers are the secondary audience but the primary growth vector
